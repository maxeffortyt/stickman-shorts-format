# Lessons from six real runs

1. **Same prompt, same story.** All six runs told nearly the same story: a Persian builder, a town that laughs at him, a dome, a shade wall, shallow pools, ice, and the same neighbours lining up in July. If you want variety, ask for it explicitly.
2. **Effort mostly bought craft, not ideas.** Low and medium drew flat 2D SVG scenes. Extra high and max built real 3D worlds in three.js with 2D characters in them, and scored highest (93.6 and 93.4). Max scored a little below extra high and cost $14 more.
3. **The voice decides the script.** The voice used here reads at about 2.5 words per second; the format runs at 3.7. Low and medium cut their scripts by a third. Opus kept a short script. High, extra high and max time-compressed the finished take (1.36×, 1.29×, 1.23×) and said so in their notes.
4. **Check the facts.** Five of six runs opened with "2,400 years ago / 400 BC", a date that is everywhere in popular articles but that we could not confirm in a serious source. Only max checked its claims online and dropped four it could not back up. Three runs ended on a true fact: in Iran a refrigerator is still called *yakhchal*, "ice pit".
5. **Fable 5.2 vs Opus 5.5.** Anthropic says Fable on high scores about level with Opus on max for roughly half the cost per task. We ran Opus one level lower (extra high): Fable high used about half the tokens, but each token costs twice as much, so the bill was about the same ($14.91 vs $12.48), while the Fable Short scored about 11 points higher (89.5 vs 78.7).
6. **Nobody can hear.** Every run wrote that it could not listen to its own mix. Budget a human listening pass.
7. **Blind your test.** Every run tried to read a shared notes folder where earlier sessions had left tips (13 attempts). They were blocked by deny rules in `.claude/settings.json`.
8. **Motion is the gap.** The format changes about a quarter of the picture every frame; the best run reached 19%.
